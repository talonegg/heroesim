### The owner's decisions (DL-144 items 4 to 11)

1. **`gm-male-03`, `temper`:** `composed` → `wry`. The owner took the checker's reading, which
   DL-143 item 2 had left open.
2. **`gm-male-20`, hook 1:** "{He} has spent years on a map of a land called Arcadia, and will not
   say whether {he} ever found it." → "{He} has spent years on a map of a land no chart names, and will
   not say whether {he} ever found it." The owner's wording, dropping "Arcadia" (DL-135 item 1).
3. **`gm-male-19`, hook 2:** "{He} keeps a shelf of sealed agreements {he} has never needed to
   open, and makes sure {his} rivals know it." → "{He} keeps a shelf of sealed agreements {he} has
   never needed to open, and makes sure everyone who deals with {him} knows it." The owner's wording.
   "Rival" is a glossary term, and the lower-case "rivals" could read as the Rival Guilds.
4. **`temperClosings.genial`, first:** "{He} laughs easily and often, and strangers find themselves
   telling {him} things they meant to keep to themselves." → "{He} is easy company, and strangers
   find themselves telling {him} things they meant to keep to themselves." The owner's wording. The
   `genial` pictures' hooks already speak of laughing (`gm-female-08`, `gm-male-10`), so a Biography
   no longer says it twice.
5. **`temperClosings.genial`, second:** "{He} greets friends and rivals with the same broad smile, and
   most of the rivals end up as friends." → "{He} greets friends and strangers with the same broad
   smile, and most of the strangers end up as friends." The owner's wording, for "rivals" as in 3.

### Hooks that rested on something worn (DL-144 item 8)

The owner asked for some of the hooks resting on clothing or armour to be replaced, so that "{He}
still wears" opens no more than two or three, keeping the best clothing readings.

- **How.** A `portrait-reader` (Opus) looked again at each of the 16 pictures whose hook rests on
  something worn. Each rated the worn hook from 1 to 5 and drafted a replacement from the room, an
  object in view or the face. A `portrait-checker` (Opus) then checked each replacement used, beside
  the picture.
- **Disputes.** The checker disputed two replacements, `gm-female-02` and `gm-male-05`, and each went
  back to its reader once. `gm-female-02`'s reader took the checker's wording. `gm-male-05`'s
  reader gave its own, meeting both of the checker's points.
- **Kept.** Two "still wears" hooks stay, as the strongest clothing readings:
  - `gm-male-01`'s leather coat: it fills the lower third of the picture;
  - `gm-male-12`'s tooled leather armour: the most worked part of the drawing, and a young man's
    first fee.

Each of these eight is the agents' wording:

6. **`gm-female-02`, hook 2:** "{He} still wears the mail {he} served in, and keeps two old helmets
   close at hand." → "{He} keeps two old helmets set out by {his} window, and will tell anyone
   who asks which of them saved {his} life." (agent's) It rests on the two helmets, one on the bench
   under the window and one on the floor. The reader rated the mail 3. The checker's dispute: the
   first draft repeated `gm-male-13`'s teacher keepsake, and "battered" said more than the picture
   shows. The reader took the checker's wording.
7. **`gm-female-07`, hook 2:** "{He} still wears the sword-belt with the knotted buckle {he} was
   given on leaving the ranks." → "The scar across {his} cheek came from a hired knife meant for
   someone else, and {he} has never told the Guild whose life it bought." (agent's) It rests on the
   diagonal scar on one cheek, which the narrative already names. Rated 2: no sword is in view, and
   the strap repeated two other strap hooks. Confirmed. The checker notes it makes a third scar
   whose story is kept back (with `gm-female-13` and `gm-male-02`), the ideas differing.
8. **`gm-female-11`, hook 2:** "{He} still wears the buckled leather strap {he} carried on the
   road, and has had the buckle mended more often than the strap." → "A pail of water stands by the
   Hall's hearth day and night, set there by {him} the winter a spark nearly took the old roof."
   (agent's) It rests on the pail at the foot of the great hearth. Rated 3: the strap is clear, but
   its road and mended buckle are not shown. Confirmed.
9. **`gm-female-14`, hook 2:** "{He} still wears a steel vambrace with {his} finest gown, a habit
   kept from years when an audience could end in a fight." → "The lion on {his} wall hanging was the
   badge of the first lord to hire {him}; the lord's line is gone, and {he} kept the hanging."
   (agent's) It rests on the rod-hung tapestry of a rampant lion. The vambrace was rated 3, the
   weaker of the picture's two worn hooks. The fang necklace was rated 4 and is kept. Confirmed. The
   checker notes that both hooks are now keepsakes of her first paid work, a hunt and a hire; one
   Biography draws at most one of them.
10. **`gm-female-17`, hook 1:** "{He} still wears the old compass that brought {him} home from the
    far fells, and trusts it before any map." → "The small bust on {his} highest shelf is of the
    scholar who first taught {him} to read a map, and {he} dusts it with {his} own hand." (agent's)
    It rests on the bust on top of the bookcase, beside the mountain painting. Rated 3: the compass
    is set on a pouch flap, not worn on its own. Confirmed.
11. **`gm-male-04`, hook 1:** "{He} still wears the mark inked beneath one eye by the company {he}
    first marched with." → "{He} bears the curled mark inked high on {his} cheek by the company
    {he} first marched with, and has outlived everyone else who took it." (agent's) It still rests on
    the face mark, rated 4, the picture's best detail. It now opens "bears", places the mark where the
    picture and narrative have it, and moves away from `gm-male-05`'s old company mark. Confirmed.
12. **`gm-male-05`, hook 1:** "{He} still wears a gorget engraved with a dragon, the mark of a
    company {he} no longer names." → "{He} came by the crossed scar high on {his} cheek from two strokes
    {he} was a breath too slow to turn, and calls it the cheapest lesson {he} ever paid for."
    (agent's) It rests on the small crossed scar the narrative names. Rated 2: the engraving is
    small, and it repeated `gm-male-03`'s dragon and `gm-male-04`'s company mark. The checker disputed
    the first draft: one cut where the picture shows a cross, and "took the short cut" misreading.
    The reader's own rewording answers both.
13. **`gm-male-15`, hook 1:** "{He} still wears the signet ring {he} once pressed into the wax of
    every contract {he} drew up." → "{He} receives every Patron beneath the great chandelier of the
    Hall, where the light falls on their faces and not on {his}." (agent's) It rests on the
    candle-ring chandelier hung from the vaulted ceiling. Rated 3, and it repeated `gm-male-02`'s
    signet ring. Confirmed. The checker notes that "great" slightly overstates a ring of about eight
    candles.
