What is drafted

Every Encounter is written in the new form: two to four named Checks, each a Query over the Party, combined by a pass formula, with consequences by Degree and harm that reads the Stats. The twelve Encounters of today are re-authored under their own names and keys; thirteen are new, four of them of the lore kind; and the four hardest templates end in a Finale of their own.

The Checks lean on the right Hero and on the weakest link rather than on headcount: most read the best Hero's Stat, the least of the Party's, or the best few with falling weights, and only the shield walls and the Mages' and Healers' joint work add Heroes up. Every Check rolls within 40% either way (today's Checks within 20%), so that a strong Party can still stumble and a weak one can still get through.

The targets were set by the calibration, each Encounter tuned so that three Heroes of Level 5 drawn at random come through it 44% of the time at difficulty 60, every Check of it as hard on its own, and each Finale so at the middle of its template's difficulties. Two fifths of each target at difficulty 60 is a flat term and the rest grows with the difficulty, so an easy Quest is harder than before and the hardest less hopeless. S10 tunes them again from its sweeps.

25 Encounters a Quest draws (12 re-authored, 13 new), and 4 Finales; 6 Items, 6 Traits and 3 Synergies new, and every Synergy written as a Query.

How to read the rates. Each is the chance estimate's, "if no one falls", for Heroes drawn by the game's own generators and raised to Level 1, 5 or 10, at steady Morale, with no gear and no Regard between them. An Encounter's rates are for Parties drawn at random, so a Check that wants a Hero of a Class is lower where none goes. A cell gives the chance of a Success or better, with the chance of a Triumph in brackets; its colour follows the chance, and its title gives all four Degrees. A target is its flat term, where it has one, plus its factor times the Quest's difficulty.

The Quest target

The target (DL-83 item 4): the player's Quests fail 20 to 30% of the time, and are strong successes at most 50%. As the estimate sees it, each template's Quests drawn by the game, 48 at each Level, met by the best Party of a Roster of 8 Heroes, if no one falls:

RosterQuestsFailSuccessStrong
Level 157616%33%51%
Level 557612%32%56%
Level 105761%18%80%

The estimate held to the resolver on 284 chances, the widest 1.2 points apart (2.4 standard errors).

In play, a 50-game sweep of 20 years with these drafts (harness/reports/m9-v114-s9-drafts-2026-10-03.md), the harness's player failed 19.9% of its Quests (10.1% with today's content), succeeded 20.5% and won 59.5% strong (74.9%). Its Heroes earned 1.34 Traits a career, the Rivals' 0.66. The failures are at the target's edge; the strong share is above it.

With the Degrees made stricter as well, which is configuration for S10 (a Triumph at 1.35 times the target in place of 1.25, and a strong success needing 60% of the Encounters it comes through to be Triumphs in place of half), the same 50 games (harness/reports/m9-v114-s9-drafts-degrees-2026-10-03.md) meet the target: failed 20.2%, succeeded 34.5% and won 45.3% strong, with 1.12 Traits a career.

The cost: 20 of the 50 player's Guilds went bankrupt with the drafts as they are, 11 of them in their first two years (2 of 50 with today's content), and 24 with the stricter Degrees, 13 in their first two years; 405 and 413 Rivals folded (178). The harness's player takes the highest-reward Quest it can staff whatever its chance, so a Guild of Level 1 Heroes takes the hardest Quests and fails them, and Upkeep takes nearly all of a Guild's income, so a few more failures tip it over. Every trial that brought failures to 20% or more bankrupted two fifths of the games or more.

Traits

Earned Traits (DL-148 item 25): a positive one is drawn on a Triumph and kept only if the Quest ends a strong success; a negative one falls only on a Hero the same failed Encounter injured.

Battle-tried #trait-battle-tried

+2 to every Check of combat Encounters · never beside Battle-shy

A Hero who has come through the worst of a fight steadies every Party they march with: +2 to every Check of a combat Encounter, once for the Party.

Earned on The last assault (a Triumph, 5%).

Wayfinder #trait-wayfinder

+2 to every Check of travel Encounters

A Hero who has found the way where there was none: +2 to every Check of a travel Encounter, once for the Party.

Earned on Lost in the fells (a Triumph, 5%).

Silver-tongued #trait-silver-tongued

+1 Diplomacy, +1 Charisma

A Hero who has talked a hard man round.

Earned on The Patron's steward (a Triumph, 5%).

Lettered #trait-lettered

+1 Intelligence, +1 Knowledge

A Hero who has read their way through a vault's secrets.

Earned on The Warden's seal (a Triumph, 5%).

Battle-shy #trait-battle-shy

-1 Courage, -1 Weaponry · never beside Battle-tried

A Hero badly hurt when the line broke, who flinches since.

Earned on Hold the line (a Failure, 8%), The last assault (a Failure, 8%).

Haunted #trait-haunted

-1 Willpower, -1 Spirit

A Hero touched by the dead, or by worse, who sleeps badly since.

Earned on A restless power (a Failure, 8%), The last rite (a Failure, 8%).

Calls for the owner

  1. Every Check rolls within 40% either way (its own variance 0.4, The answer's too) where today's roll within 20%. A proposal for S10: make it configuration, checkVariance 0.4, and keep a Check's own variance only where that Check truly differs. At 20%, a Party whose capability equals the target rolls between 0.8 and 1.2 times it, so it can only Setback (from 0.75) or succeed (below the Triumph's 1.25); at 40% it rolls between 0.6 and 1.4 times it, so all four Degrees can happen. With 20%, Heroes of Level 10 passed everything and Heroes of Level 1 almost nothing.
  2. The targets are set by the calibration (HEROESIM_TUNE=0.44 HEROESIM_FLAT=0.4): each Encounter so that three random Heroes of Level 5 come through it 44% of the time at difficulty 60, every Check of it as hard on its own; each Finale at the middle of its template's difficulties; two fifths of each target a flat term. Without the flat term the vault's hardest third failed 92% at Level 1 and 94% at Level 5; with it, 60% and 52%. S10 tunes from there.
  3. The Checks read the best Hero, the weakest Hero or the best few with falling weights; only the shield walls (the three sturdiest) and the Mages' and Healers' joint work add Heroes up, so a large Party helps less than before.
  4. A Check that wants a Hero of a Class reads none without one: the Diagnosis (a Healer), the Unbinding (the Mages), the Command of the fort's last assault (a Warrior), The rite (a Healer) and The lock (Lockpicking). Each sits in an 'or' with another Check, or on a template that requires that Hero.
  5. The sickness at the mill draws social and lore Encounters, not social and arcane; Lay the restless dead, Survey the old workings and Break the sealed vault draw lore Encounters as well.
  6. Two new tags: holy (Church work) and study (scholars' work). The Encounter tags follow the templates' vocabulary otherwise.
  7. Each positive Trait is earned on one Encounter's Triumph, at 5%: Battle-tried on The last assault; Wayfinder on Lost in the fells; Silver-tongued on The Patron's steward; Lettered on The Warden's seal. The two negative Traits, at 8%, follow an Injury: Battle-shy on Hold the line and The last assault, Haunted on A restless power and The last rite. Battle-shy and Battle-tried exclude each other.
  8. The harm table: a blow falls likelier on the slow (Dexterity) and lands less on the hardy (Constitution); a fall or a trap on the unwatchful (Perception), less on the nimble (Dexterity); fever on the weary (Endurance), less on the hardy (Constitution); magic on the unresisting, less on the resisting (Resistance). A Death is guarded by the Stat of its blow. Morale losses are steadied by Courage and Willpower (dread), Endurance and Willpower (hardship), Adaptability (shame) or Willpower (a puzzle).
  9. The six new Items are all trinkets, each priced a little below its tier's core trinket (35, 100 to 115 and 280 against 40, 120 and 300): the automatic buyer takes the dearest piece it can afford, the lowest key on a tie, so a piece in the armour slot, or at a core price, would take the place of the Constitution armour the harness's player and the Rivals buy. With six more Items for sale, the Market's ten stacks a day hold each Item less often (10 of 21 in place of 10 of 15); S10 may raise marketSize.
  10. For the owner: the Quest target is met by the drafts with the stricter Degrees (20.2% failed, 45.3% strong), but only with 24 of the 50 harness Guilds bankrupt, 13 of them in their first two years (2 of 50 with today's content). Either the harness's player chooses its Quests by their chance (say, never one its best Party finds Hopeless or Unlikely), or the economy is looked at before Random Events, or the target waits. S10 needs that answer.
